Academic Research and Information Sources

1. Why Wikipedia is not enough

Three reasons Wikipedia should not be used as an academic source:

  1. Wikipedia content is not necessarily written by subject experts, and may simply be incorrect.
  2. Articles may be changed or deleted between viewings — what you cite today may not be there tomorrow.
  3. Anyone can search Google or find Wikipedia. To develop real academic skill you have to go beyond these basic tools.

2. The safe way to use Wikipedia

Wikipedia is not forbidden — it is just not a source. Use it as a starting point, in two steps:

  1. Scan the article to get general information and to pick up the vocabulary and key terms of the topic.
  2. Scan the article for its references, then go and read those — those are the things you can actually cite.

3. Google Scholar

Google Scholar

A service that provides a simple way to search for scholarly articles from one place.

Features

  • Search all scholarly articles from one place.
  • Explore related works, citations, authors and publications.
  • Locate the complete document across the web.
  • Keep up with recent developments in any area of research.
  • Check who is citing your publication.

4. arXiv

arXiv

A free distribution service and open-access archive holding around two million scholarly articles in the fields of physics, mathematics, computer science and others.
